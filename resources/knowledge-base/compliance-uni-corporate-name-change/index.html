Corporate Name Change Procedures

Coordinate name approval, corporate authorization and registry filing using the rules for your corporation. A word name and an assigned numbered name do not follow the same search process.

Alberta government fees

  • Alberta corporation name/structure change, including amendment, arrangement, reorganization or restatement: CAD $53.05.

Registry agent service charges and any required name-search or professional fees are separate. Ask your provider for an itemised quote; fees and exemptions depend on the entity and transaction.

Source: Government of Alberta, Registry Agent Product Catalogue, pp. 14–16. Effective October 1, 2026; checked October 16, 2026. Confirm the current catalogue before filing.

Start with the Governing Jurisdiction

Identify where the corporation is incorporated or continued. Extra-provincial registrations may also need updating after the home-jurisdiction name change. Changing a corporation's legal name is different from registering a trade name or changing a domain name.

Do not assume that every change requires a separately ordered NUANS report, a 90-day reservation, or 21 days' meeting notice. Those details depend on the jurisdiction, name type and approval route.

Alberta Business Corporations

For a proposed word name, obtain the Alberta NUANS report required by the current amendment instructions. It must be less than 91 days old when submitted. An assigned numbered name does not require that name-search report.

Authorize the amendment and take the applicable Articles of Amendment, name-search documents, identification and fees to an authorized service provider. The government amendment fee is CAD $53.05, plus provider and any search charges.

Alberta name-change guide →

Federal Business Corporations (CBCA)

For a word name in Articles of Amendment, follow Corporations Canada's online name pre-approval process. An approved name is reserved for 90 days, measured from the pre-approval request date. Do not apply the integrated online incorporation name-search process to every amendment.

Obtain the required shareholder authorization and file Articles of Amendment. The ordinary online business-corporation amendment fee is $200. Other types of amendment and filing channels can have different fees or exemptions.

Ontario Business Corporations

Use the Ontario Business Registry's current Articles of Amendment requirements, including Ontario-biased NUANS documentation where applicable to a proposed word name. Check the instructions for numbered names and other exceptions.

The government fee for Articles of Amendment is $150. Confirm the current submission channel, required supporting information and any intermediary charges before filing.

British Columbia Companies

BC uses its own Name Request and approval process. An approved name is reserved for 56 days; a numbered company name does not need name reservation. A NUANS report is not a universal substitute for BC name approval.

The current company-name-change fee is $100, with a separate $30 name-approval fee where applicable. Follow the company's authorization requirements and keep its annual reports up to date.

Plan the Approval and Filing Sequence

  1. Check the proposed name. Review the registry's name rules, search or approval route and expiry date. Consider trademarks and confusingly similar names; domain availability alone does not establish a legal right to use a corporate name.
  2. Obtain the required corporate approval. A voluntary legal-name amendment generally requires a shareholder special resolution. Confirm the applicable voting threshold, quorum and any separate class vote. A two-thirds threshold refers to the votes cast by those entitled to vote, rather than automatically two-thirds of every shareholder.
  3. Use the correct notice or written-resolution route. Meeting notice depends on the governing statute, bylaws and any valid waiver. For Alberta, ABCA s.134 generally specifies 21–50 days, while a non-reporting corporation's bylaws may provide 7–60 days under s.134(1.1). Written resolutions and waived notice have their own conditions; 21 days is not a universal minimum for every name change.
  4. File before the applicable name approval expires. Use the correct registry form or online filing, corporate identifier and required supporting documents. Do not assume every registry requires a certified resolution and a NUANS report as attachments. Retain the authorization in the corporate records.
  5. Confirm registration and coordinate updates. Check the resulting certificate or registry confirmation and effective date. Update the minute book, extra-provincial registrations, tax records, banks, licences and counterparties as applicable. Review contract notice requirements rather than assuming every existing agreement must be rewritten.

Timing, Fees and Name Decisions

Approval expiry and registry processing are different timelines. Build in time for shareholder approval and any corrections before the reservation expires. Use the registry's current service standard for the chosen filing channel; there is no uniform 5–15-business-day processing period across Canada.

A search report identifies potentially conflicting names; the registry decides whether the proposed name meets its requirements. If a name is rejected, review the reasons and consider a more distinctive alternative. Changing only “Inc.” to “Ltd.” does not necessarily resolve confusion.

Government fees above were checked on October 15, 2026 and exclude separate service-provider, search, professional and applicable tax charges. Other provinces, Quebec entities, non-profits and registry-directed name changes need their own procedures.
