Alberta Plan of Arrangement / Articles of Arrangement

A plan of arrangement is a court-approved corporate transaction under ABCA s.193. The corporation sends the court order and applicable transaction documents to Corporate Registry; the documents and certificate depend on what the arrangement does.

Alberta government fees

  • File a plan of arrangement without amendment for an Alberta corporation: CAD $0.00.
  • Alberta corporation name/structure change, including amendment, arrangement, reorganization or restatement: CAD $53.05.

Registry agent service charges and any required name-search or professional fees are separate. Ask your provider for an itemised quote; fees and exemptions depend on the entity and transaction.

Overview

A plan of arrangement lets an Alberta corporation reorganize under court supervision. It's used for major transactions (e.g. mergers, share exchanges, divisive reorganizations or debt restructurings) that affect shareholder or creditor interests. The board and/or shareholders initiate the process under ABCA s.193.

The court may make interim and final orders governing notice, meetings, approval and the transaction. Send the order and applicable documents under s.193(4.1). A plan without amendment has a CAD $0.00 government fee; an arrangement changing the company's name or structure has a CAD $53.05 government fee, plus separate provider charges.

Under s.193(11)–(12), the arrangement takes effect on the date of the appropriate certificate, if one is required, or on the filing date if no certificate is required. Follow the court order and the applicable statutory filing requirements.

Step-by-Step Checklist

1. Authorize and Draft the Plan

Prepare the proposed transaction and confirm that s.193 is available: under s.193(3), if another provision can effect the transaction, an arrangement application requires that other route to be impracticable. A corporation, security holder or creditor may apply under s.193(2).

2. Obtain Court Approval

Apply to the Court of King's Bench under s.193. Give the Registrar notice of the application under s.193(3.1). Follow the court's directions on notice, meetings, voting and any dissent rights; do not assume a uniform vote or notice waiver.

3. Gather Required Forms

Under s.193(4.1), send the court order and, where applicable, articles of arrangement, articles of amalgamation, a statement of intent to dissolve, and documents required by ss.20 and 113. Use REG3059 when articles of arrangement are applicable; do not require every notice for every transaction.

4. Submit Filings to the Corporate Registry

Submit the order and applicable documents as required by the order and the Act; s.193 does not set a universal 60-day filing period. A plan without amendment has a CAD $0.00 government fee; a name/structure amendment by arrangement has a CAD $53.05 fee. Provider charges are separate.

5. Receive Certificate and Update Records

The Registrar issues the appropriate certificate, if any, under s.193(11). Record the effective date under s.193(12), retain the filed documents and update the minute book, share records and other affected registrations.

6. Regulator Contact

For filing instructions and contact details, use the official Alberta amendment guidance or contact Corporate Registry at 780-427-7013.

Key Legal Basis

Business Corporations Act (Alberta), RSA 2000 c B‑9 – Part on Arrangements

Court powers are in s.193(4); applicable documents accompany the order under s.193(4.1). Subsections 193(11)–(12) govern the certificate, if any, and effective date. Former ss.193(9)–(10) are repealed and are not current filing authority.

The court determines the necessary notice and meetings under s.193(4). If a meeting is ordered, s.193(7) permits a unanimous written resolution by those entitled to vote instead of that meeting.

Forms and Filings

Use Articles of Arrangement (REG3059) when applicable, together with the order and other documents required for the actual transaction. The official forms directory supplies the current forms.

Other Statutory References

Directors' and officers' duties are addressed in s.122. The court may permit dissent under s.193(4)(d), engaging s.191. Confirm the order and the transaction before assuming a dissent right or approval threshold.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a "plan of arrangement" and the "articles of arrangement"?

The plan describes the proposed transaction. The articles of arrangement are a filing document used where applicable to implement the approved transaction, accompanying the court order under s.193(4.1).

Is court approval required for an arrangement?

Yes. Alberta law requires that any plan of arrangement be approved by the Alberta Court of King's Bench under ABCA s.193. The court reviews the fairness and compliance of the arrangement before granting a final order.

Do shareholders need to approve the arrangement?

Follow the court's order under s.193(4) for meetings, voting and affected classes. If the court orders a meeting, a unanimous written resolution of those entitled to vote can replace it under s.193(7). Do not assume every arrangement follows an ordinary special-resolution vote.

When does the arrangement become effective?

Section 193(12) provides that the arrangement is effective on the certificate date, or the filing date if no certificate is required. Implement the court-approved transaction using the applicable documents and record that statutory effective date.
