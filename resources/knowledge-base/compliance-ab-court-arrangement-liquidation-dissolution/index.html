Court-Ordered Arrangement, Liquidation, or Dissolution Under ABCA

Court-approved restructuring and wind-up procedures have different applicants, powers and filing requirements. Start with the statutory route and the terms of the court order.

Official Sources

Publisher: Good Standing AI · Jurisdiction: Alberta business corporations under the ABCA

Sources checked: October 15, 2026. Scope: arrangements, standing, liquidation powers and procedural approvals under ss.193, 215–224 and 239–243.

  • Alberta King’s Printer: Business Corporations Act, current as of September 2, 2026
  • Government of Alberta: Corporate Registry forms
  • Government of Alberta: liquidation and dissolution filing guidance
Good Standing AI is not a law firm and does not provide legal advice. This guide is general information. Qualified Alberta counsel should assess standing, available relief, evidence and the court process for the particular matter.

Arrangements — ABCA s.193

Section 193 covers a range of arrangements, including amendments, amalgamations, transfers, compromises and liquidation and dissolution. Under s.193(2), the corporation, a security holder or a creditor can apply. Section 193(3) restricts use of this route where the arrangement can practicably be effected under another provision of the Act.

The court can direct notice, meetings, voting procedures and other terms through interim or final orders. Give the Registrar notice of the application. Do not assume that the same meeting sequence or approval threshold applies to every arrangement.

After an order under s.193(4), send a copy to the Registrar with the documents applicable under s.193(4.1), which may include Articles of Arrangement, Articles of Amalgamation, a Statement of Intent to Dissolve or address/director documents. Under s.193(12), effectiveness follows the certificate date, or the filing date where no certificate is required.

Shareholder Liquidation Applications — ABCA s.215

Section 215(1) permits a shareholder application on the grounds it specifies, including oppressive or unfairly prejudicial conduct, a qualifying unanimous shareholder agreement provision, or just and equitable grounds. The court has discretion over the relief.

Section 215(2) also permits orders under s.242, and s.215(3) applies the procedures in s.243. A creditor is not automatically a shareholder applicant under s.215(1); any alternative route and standing must be assessed separately.

Oppression Relief and Standing — ABCA ss.239 and 242

A complainant under s.239 can seek relief under s.242. The definition includes specified security holders, directors and officers, and persons the court considers proper applicants. A creditor seeking s.242 relief needs the court to exercise the discretion identified in s.239(b)(iii)(B) and (iv).

The powers in s.242(3) include liquidation and dissolution under clause (n). Section 242(8) allows an applicant to seek liquidation and dissolution in the alternative under s.215(1)(a). These provisions explain why a creditor may have a potential route without making every creditor a direct s.215 shareholder applicant.

Procedural Approvals — ABCA s.243

Section 243 addresses procedural matters, including court approval for staying, discontinuing or settling proceedings, dismissal for want of prosecution and interim costs. It is not the source of the general oppression-remedy powers listed in s.242.

Following the Court Order

  1. Confirm the applicant's standing, the statutory route, required notices and the exact relief requested.
  2. Follow the court-directed notice, meeting, evidence and approval process. An interim order may set the steps before final approval.
  3. For court-supervised liquidation, follow the order and the applicable provisions in ss.216–224. Section 221 deals with appointment of a liquidator, and s.222 sets duties including notices.
  4. Submit the court order and the registry documents required for that route. A liquidation and dissolution process must reach the applicable completion stage; a court order does not make every corporation immediately dissolved.
  5. Retain the order, filing confirmations and resulting certificate. Check the current registry tariff for each applicable filing, separately from court and professional costs.

The order and applicable statutory provisions control timing. There is no universal “file within a few weeks” deadline for all arrangements and liquidations.

Forms and Related Procedures

Use the official Corporate Registry forms index to select current Articles of Arrangement, Articles of Dissolution and any applicable liquidator or address/director notices. Some government PDFs require Adobe Reader.

An ordinary voluntary wind-up may instead use ABCA s.211 or s.212. Section 213 governs Registrar dissolution on its specified grounds; court-ordered dissolution under s.214 and shareholder applications under s.215 have separate legal bases.

See also plans of arrangement and articles of reorganization.
